# Study guide: Hydrogen

## What this area is about

Hydrogen is the one atom quantum mechanics solves exactly. In this area you use those exact solutions to see where orbital shapes, energy levels and spectral lines come from, and how a superposition makes an atom's electron cloud move. Every picture in the Hydrogen views is computed from the formulas in the lectures, not drawn by hand.

**Who it is for:** students of physics, chemistry and engineering, and anyone curious why atoms look the way they do. It is optional for quantum computing, but it shows the same quantum rules at work in a real system.

**Time:** about 7–8 hours — roughly 45 minutes per lecture and per lab.

## Before you start

You should be comfortable with the ideas of [Superposition and the Bloch sphere](qs:lesson/principles/01-superposition-and-bloch-sphere) and [Measurement and the Born rule](qs:lesson/principles/02-measurement-and-born-rule). Useful mathematics:

- [Complex numbers](qs:lesson/math/01-complex-numbers)
- [Eigenvalues](qs:lesson/math/05-eigenvalues)
- [Probability](qs:lesson/math/06-probability)

Calculus helps for the radial probability, but every result is also given in words and numbers.

## How to study each lecture

1. Read **The idea** and predict what the experiment will show.
2. Redo the **Worked example** with a calculator before reading its numbers.
3. Open the views in **In the app** and compare them with your prediction.
4. Answer the four questions; reread the section behind any you miss.
5. Read **Common mistakes** last and check you would not make them.

## Check yourself at the end

You have finished this area when you can:

- give the allowed ℓ and m for any n, and the number of radial and angular nodes;
- explain why the 1s density peaks at the nucleus but the radial probability peaks at $a_\mu$;
- compute $E_n$ and the wavelength of any transition, and say whether it is allowed;
- compute a beat period $T = h/|E_a - E_b|$ and explain what the animation shows;
- name three effects the model leaves out and their rough sizes.
